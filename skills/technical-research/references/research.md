# Source policy

Investigate one question against high-trust primary sources and write a cited Markdown artifact.

## Evidence rules

1. Prefer sources that own the fact: official documentation, source code, specifications, standards, first-party APIs, and original papers or announcements.
2. Use secondary sources to discover claims and primary sources. Do not treat repetition across secondary sources as independent confirmation.
3. Check the source date, version, scope, and status. A correct claim for one release can be wrong for another.
4. Trace a secondary claim to its cited primary source. If that source does not support the claim, record the conflict instead of repeating the claim.
5. Cross-check consequential claims when two independent primary sources exist.
6. Record facts, conflicts, uncertainty, version or date limits, and unanswered questions.
7. Cite each material claim with a URL, source path and line, API response, paper section, or specification section.

When sources conflict, prefer the source with direct authority over the exact fact. Do not silently merge incompatible claims. State each claim, identify its source, explain the authority or scope difference, and say what remains unresolved.

Keep quotations short. Summarize the evidence in your own words and preserve the source's technical meaning.
