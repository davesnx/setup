# Before/after examples

## Example 1: Throat-clearing and binary contrast

**Before:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**
> "Building products is hard because people are complex, not because the technology is complex."

**Changes:** Removed the opener and emphasis crutch. Kept the contrast because it carries the original claim.

---

## Example 2: Filler and unnecessary reassurance

**Before:**
> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**
> "Most teams struggle with alignment. Nobody wants to admit confusion, although being confused is acceptable."

**Changes:** Preserved the quantifier, both observations, and the conclusion while removing throat-clearing.

---

## Example 3: Business jargon stack

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**
> "The situation changes quickly. We need to accept discomfort and handle uncertainty with clarity because your competition is not waiting."

**Changes:** Replaced business jargon while retaining each original claim.

---

## Example 4: Dramatic fragmentation

**Before:**
> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**
> "You can choose only two of speed, quality, and cost."

**Changes:** Single sentence. No performative emphasis.

---

## Example 5: Rhetorical setup

**Before:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**
> "The best teams optimize for learning rather than productivity."

**Changes:** Direct claim. No rhetorical scaffolding.
