# Resolve gaps in an incomplete post

Work from the draft, source material, prior answers, and `VOICE.md` if present.
Keep progress in working notes and unresolved questions in the text. This process
does not require a site, frontmatter format, or status block.

## 1. Map the gaps

A gap is something the reader needs that the post does not yet provide. Find:

- Broken sentences, `xxx`, TODOs, `[Q: ...]`, and TBD placeholders.
- Missing context, explanations, consequences, or attribution.
- Claims whose evidence is absent or too narrow for their scope.
- Titles and headings that promise an answer the body does not give.

For each gap, record its location, what is missing, and how to resolve it:

- **Source fact:** Check the repository, linked issue, documentation, benchmark,
  or project history. Name where you will look. Research it before asking the author.
- **Author input:** Ask for an unavailable personal experience, motive, cost,
  preference, or decision. Reuse answers already supplied or recorded in notes.

Split a mixed gap into its factual and author parts. Read relevant linked material
and check whether another section already supplies the answer. The map is ready
when each known gap has a location and a next source or question.

## 2. Resolve facts and ask focused questions

Investigate source facts. Keep checked evidence separate from assumptions and from
what another source's author experienced. If a source is unavailable, record what
is needed and where it should come from.

Ask the author only for unresolved input whose prerequisites are settled. Group
related questions in numbered rounds. Include the section or line and what the
reader needs there. Invite shorthand, links, and pasted terminal output.

When known context supports a tentative answer, label it as a guess for correction.
Otherwise ask the question directly. A guess is not evidence until confirmed.
Use open questions rather than forcing anecdotes or decisions into fixed choices.

After each answer or source, update the gap map. A correction can open a new gap;
a link can resolve one without another question. Keep working on independent gaps
while an answer is missing. Stop asking when no material gap needs author input,
but continue to track unresolved facts.

## 3. Write supported additions

Write each resolved passage where it belongs, within the agreed editing scope.
Use confirmed answers or inspected evidence; no completed interview round is
required. Respect any section approval steps the author requested.

- Use the author's nouns and verbs, with the context the reader needs.
- Preserve uncertainty and scope: “might”, “in my case”, and “usually” carry meaning.
- Check each addition against its source and any relevant voice examples.
- Keep unavailable evidence explicit with `[Q: what is missing and where to obtain it]`.
- Reread the working file before editing and preserve unrelated material.

Show changed passages rather than reprinting the whole article. Recheck the gap
map after writing: an addition may answer several questions or expose another one.

If a source or answer remains unavailable, write the supported parts and report a
partial draft with the outstanding questions and evidence needed. A promised
measurement is not a result. A supported uncertain or mixed conclusion can be
complete when it answers the post's actual question. Then continue with the
writing skill's final polish and completion checks.
