# Review framework

Judge the post against its purpose, intended reader, and requested voice. A useful
explanation, personal account, exploratory essay, and persuasive argument need
different things. Apply the relevant checks and report concrete issues, without
requiring a finding for each section below. An outline's missing prose is not a defect.

## Purpose, opening, and ending

Identify what the post offers and whether it delivers. Check for a mismatch between
the title, opening, body, and ending. Name the specific promise left unanswered
rather than asking for a stronger hook by default.

A quiet opening can provide useful context. A detailed explanation can take time.
An ending can report a result, state a limit, leave a question open, or simply stop
when the work is done. Flag unsupported conclusions or abandoned promises, not the
absence of drama, a summary, direct address, or a call to action. Sharing potential
is relevant only when the author made it a goal.

## Order and repetition

Check whether concepts arrive before the reader needs them and whether a missing
step prevents a conclusion from following. Preserve the author's reasoning and
sequence when they work; a standard story arc is not a requirement.

For repetition, compare both passages and identify what the second adds. A reminder,
refrain, or return to an earlier question may be useful. Propose a cut or merge when
the later passage adds nothing to the reader's understanding or experience.

## Claims and support

Check factual accuracy, attribution, assumptions, and the limits of a claim.
Distinguish an error from a claim that could not be verified. A citation is not
proof until its contents support the passage.

| Support | Check |
|---|---|
| Personal experience | Does the claim stay within that experience, or become a general rule? |
| Reasoning or interpretation | Are the premises explicit and does the conclusion follow? |
| Study, dataset, or named source | Does the source support the claim's scope, conditions, and date? |
| Expert quotation | Is it attributed correctly, and does it support this claim? |
| Specific example | Is the example accurate, and what can one case establish? |
| Vague attribution or unsupported assertion | What evidence is needed, and would qualification change the claim? |

Opinions and personal reactions need not be converted into measured results.
Inspect code, captions, units, and output when they support the argument; state
verification limits instead of treating an unrun example as checked.

## Clarity for this reader

Find ambiguous references, unexplained prerequisites, overloaded sentences, and
abstract passages where the reader cannot tell what happened or why. Keep technical
terms the intended audience needs. Depth and difficulty inherent in the topic are
not writing errors; identify the avoidable obstacle.

## Voice and editorial restraint

Compare with the requested voice, `VOICE.md`, and relevant author samples. Flag
generic replacements that erased a specific observation, uncertainty, humour,
or an aside. Preserve unusual but clear wording and deliberate rhythms.

Explain any proposed style change through its effect on this passage. Sentence
length, paragraph length, punctuation, or a phrase associated with AI writing is
not sufficient evidence of a defect. Judge the writing, not an AI-detector score.
